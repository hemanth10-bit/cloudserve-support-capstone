# Decision record

Short entries, newest last. Each says what was decided, why, and what evidence supports it.

## D-01 Python 3.12 and re-resolved dependencies (2026-11-10, B-01)
- **Decision:** develop and run on Python 3.12 (3.10+ is the documented minimum). Dependencies are declared in `requirements.in`; `requirements.txt` is the pinned lock generated from a working install.
- **Why:** the supplied `requirements.txt` does not install on Python 3.12. A dry run failed building `pandas==2.0.0` (no 3.12 wheel, source build fails with `No module named 'pkg_resources'`). Other pins (numpy 1.24.0, scikit-learn 1.3.0, chromadb 0.3.21, langchain 0.1.0) are similarly too old for a current Python.
- **Left out:** `psycopg2-binary` (decision log uses SQLite), `jupyter`/`ipython` (no notebooks; a notebook is not a system), `aiohttp`, `langchain-community`.
- **Check:** `pip install -r requirements.txt` into a fresh venv, then `python -m pytest tests/`.

## D-02 Orchestration (2026-11-10, B-01)
- **Decision:** use `langgraph` for the ingest -> classify -> retrieve -> route -> generate -> validate graph and the OpenAI SDK pointed at OpenRouter for model calls. No `langchain-community` or `langchain-openai`.
- **Why:** keeps the dependency surface small and the pipeline explicit and testable, while still using LangGraph as the brief suggests.

## D-03 Classifier is not an LLM (2026-11-10, Stage 3)
- **Decision:** intent and urgency come from MiniLM embeddings plus a calibrated logistic regression trained on the development tickets.
- **Why:** deterministic (A5), free, no rate limits over a full unattended run (A9), and gives real probabilities for calibration.

## D-04 Chunking and retrieval design (2026-11-10, B-03/B-04)
- **Decision:** one chunk per article section (overview, symptoms, common causes, resolution, notes), title prefixed to the embedded text; 29 articles -> 145 chunks. Sections are never split, so numbered resolution steps stay whole. Chroma (cosine) holds the vectors; hits roll up to the article (best chunk score); citations are article ids (`DOC-...`), chunk ids (`DOC-...#section`) resolve to passages.
- **Why:** articles are short and uniformly structured; customers describe symptoms, which the symptoms chunk matches. Whole-article chunks would dilute the match and exceed MiniLM's input length.
- **Evidence (dev set, 357 answerable tickets):** the expected article is ranked first for 88.8%, in the top 3 for 94.7% and in the top 5 for 98.9% (MRR 0.926). The worked example from the Ines interview ("my deployment keeps dying") returns DOC-DEPLOY-001, the article she names, which a title keyword search cannot reach. Weak spots at top 3: onboarding 70%, deployment_failure 74%, integration_help 81%.

## D-05 Relevance threshold 0.40, and what retrieval cannot do (2026-11-10, B-04)
- **Decision:** `RETRIEVAL_MIN_SCORE=0.40`. Passages below it are dropped. At 0.40 the expected article is still in the top 5 for 98.0% of answerable tickets (98.9% with no threshold).
- **Finding:** retrieval score does not separate answerable from unanswerable tickets (median top score 0.643 vs 0.635). Unanswerable includes 108 tickets that are on-topic but not answerable from the article, and feature requests score high (median 0.72) because they are on-topic. Unclear requests score low (max 0.40).
- **Consequence:** answerability cannot be decided by retrieval score. Feature, unclear, security and compliance tickets are kept off the auto-answer path by the intent rule (FR-05), and the generator's `can_answer=false` path and the validator catch unsupported answers. The PRD acceptance criterion for FR-03 ("feature and unclear tickets return no passages") is replaced by intent gating; recorded for the Stage 5 revision log.
- **Sweep and rows:** `evaluation/results/retrieval_eval.json` (re-create with `python -m evaluation.retrieval_eval`).

## D-06 Classifier: features, grouping, calibration (2026-11-10, B-06)
- **Decision:** MiniLM embeddings of subject + body -> logistic regression (intent, C=100 chosen by out-of-fold log-loss) with temperature scaling fitted on out-of-fold logits (T=0.69). Urgency uses the calibrated intent probabilities as features (chosen over embeddings alone by out-of-fold log-loss) with its own temperature (T=1.15). Model and report are saved in `models/` and the model is retrained deterministically if missing. Re-create with `python -m src.classify`.
- **Mistake caught on the way:** the first run grouped folds on subject + body and reported 99.0% intent accuracy. The same body appears under different subjects, so those folds leaked. Grouping on body alone gives 95.8%, which is the figure to quote. Recorded because the leakage was easy to miss and the number looked fine.
- **Honest accuracy (development, folds grouped by body text, 215 groups):** intent accuracy 95.8%, macro precision 96.0%, macro recall 95.5%; calibration error 0.035 (0.077 before calibration). Stricter lower bound with near-duplicate paraphrases kept together (cosine >= 0.85, 125 groups): 89.6%. Weakest classes by recall: api_key_issue 61%, compliance_request 81%, api_usage_question 88%.
- **Must-escalate safety:** out-of-fold, all 87 always-escalate tickets were predicted as an always-escalate class (0 missed). The router also uses `p_must_escalate`, the probability mass on those four classes.
- **Urgency is barely predictable:** 49.8% out of fold against a 45.2% majority baseline, mean confidence 0.52, calibration error 0.036. Best possible from text is about 73% because identical texts carry different urgency labels. Urgency confidence is therefore honest and low; the report must say urgency is a weak signal and must not be presented as reliable prioritisation.
- **Validation caveat:** the classifier is trained on all 500 development tickets, and 62 of 80 validation texts occur in development. Validation intent accuracy (100% on 80, including 18 unseen texts) is optimistic and is reported split by seen/unseen text.

## D-07 Pipeline and harness (2026-11-10, B-05)
- `src/pipeline.py` processes one ticket and never raises; `evaluation/harness.py --input X --output DIR` processes every ticket in any file with the ticket schema, writes `results.jsonl` incrementally, then `metrics.json` and `metrics.md`. Exit code 0 = completed and reconciled, 1 = completed but counts differ, 2 = input unreadable.
- **Status:** the router is a placeholder (replaced in B-07) and generation/validation are not built (B-08/B-09). Reports from this stage carry a PROVISIONAL banner and must not be quoted. On validation the placeholder answers 81% and escalates 19%, whereas the labels say 40% should escalate (route agreement 76%): the placeholder ignores whether a ticket is actually answerable, which only generation (`can_answer`) and the validator can judge.

## D-08 Router, answerability estimate and threshold (2026-11-10, B-07)
- **Decision:** deterministic rule order R1 (classifier unavailable) -> R2 (always-escalate class: security, compliance, feature, unclear) -> R3 (probability mass on those classes >= 0.20) -> R4 (retrieval failed or empty) -> R5 (estimated answerability below threshold) -> auto-respond. The rule that fired and a plain-language reason are recorded on every ticket. Code: `src/router.py`; fitted values: `models/router.json`; re-fit with `python -m src.router`.
- **Answerability estimate:** p_auto = sum over intents of P(intent) x (share of development tickets of that intent labelled "should auto-respond"), zero for the four always-escalate classes. Chosen over a learned model because it is readable by a support manager and was as good: with leak-free folds the best learned alternatives reached AUC about 0.64 on the 413 tickets the classifier considers safe-class, and the simple per-intent rate gave similar precision at the same coverage.
- **Evidence it is only a coarse filter:** among those 413 tickets the base rate of "should auto-respond" is 75.3%. Retrieval scores and embeddings add almost nothing, because identical texts carry both labels (data_findings.md point 3). So no threshold makes this stage precise; generation (`can_answer`) and the validator must carry the rest of the protection.
- **Threshold = 0.64, from a stated cost argument and not a default.** Answer when p_auto x saving > (1 - p_auto) x W, so threshold = W / (W + 1). W is the cost that actually reaches the customer: a wrong reply costs 9 savings (assumption, reflecting "I would rather it said nothing", Marcus) multiplied by the share of wrongly routed tickets assumed to survive generation and validation (20%, a **placeholder**, to be measured at B-09 and B-11). W = 1.8, threshold 0.64.
- **Sweep (development, out-of-fold):** threshold 0.60 -> 68.2% of tickets auto-routed, 79.5% of them labelled correct; 0.65 -> 62.8%, 79.9%; 0.70 -> 48.2%, 80.1%; 0.75 -> 36.8%, 84.8%; 0.80 -> 26.0%, 86.9%. A stricter threshold such as 0.75 (W = 3) would auto-answer only 37% of tickets, below today's 42% first-contact resolution, so it cannot meet the business goal; 0.64 keeps about 63-68% coverage and drops the intents that need diagnosis (database_issue 39% answerable, performance_degradation 44%, configuration_help 58%).
- **Safety check:** zero always-escalate tickets were auto-routed at any threshold in the sweep, and none on the 500 development or 80 validation tickets end to end (test_router.py). The R3 limit (0.20) made no difference between 0.10 and 0.50 on development; it stays as a margin.
- **Validation run with the real router (optimistic: 62 of 80 texts seen in development):** 58 routed to auto-respond, 22 escalated; of the 58, 43 are labelled "should auto-respond" and 15 are labelled not answerable. Route agreement with the labels is 75.0%. Those 15 are what generation and the validator must stop.
- **Open (PRD question 4):** billing commitments and data-residency claims have no separate rule yet; they are only covered through intent rates (billing_query 85%, data_residency 71% answerable). A wording check belongs in the B-09 validator.

## D-09 Decision log (2026-11-11, B-10)
- **Decision:** SQLite via SQLAlchemy (`src/decision_log.py`), one row per decision with the Governance Framework minimum fields plus the router rule and requirement ids. Stages: classification and routing for every ticket (including failed and malformed ones); generation and validation rows are added when those stages run (B-08, B-09). Each harness run writes its own `decisions.db` in the output folder (reset per run, so reruns never double count); the API uses `DATABASE_URL`.
- **Reconciliation (A8):** `reconcile()` compares logged rows with tickets processed per ticket and stage and lists every gap; the harness metrics report prints it and the harness exit code is 1 if it does not reconcile. Validation run: 160 of 160 expected decisions logged, 0 missing, 0 write failures.
- **Never stops a run:** if the database write fails the row goes to a `.fallback.jsonl` file, the failure is counted and shown in the report.
- **Privacy:** input_summary has channel, tier, length, a SHA-256 prefix and a 120-character excerpt; no customer name or id (tested).
- **Deviation from the schema's illustration:** `action_taken` is "continue" on a normal classification row (classifying takes no customer-facing action) and "escalate" when classification fell back. Routing and validation rows use the schema's auto_respond / escalate / block.

## D-10 Generation and hand-over notes (2026-11-11, B-08)
- **Decision:** `src/llm.py` (OpenAI SDK pointed at OpenRouter, `meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct`, temperature 0, SQLite response cache, own retry with exponential backoff and jitter for 429/5xx/timeouts only) and `src/generation.py` (PR-01 drafts the reply, PR-02 writes the hand-over note). A ticket the router sends to auto-respond is still escalated if drafting declines, fails or cites a document that was not retrieved. Every escalated ticket gets a hand-over note, from a template if the model fails. Prompts moved to v0.2 only to get real placeholders and a SYSTEM/USER split; the rules are unchanged.
- **Citations are checked in code,** not trusted: every cited id must be among the passages retrieved for that ticket and a reply with none is rejected.
- **Measured on 60 development tickets (distinct texts), 2026-11-11:** 40 sent to drafting, 40 drafted, 0 declined, 0 model errors, 0 unparseable, 0 bad citations; 18 of 40 replies had at least one sentence without an inline `[DOC-...]` tag (the model follows the citation list but not the sentence-level rule); 6 of 40 were labelled not answerable. The model never declined, so `can_answer` is not a useful filter on its own and the validator carries the load.
- **Why a cache:** reruns of the same tickets give the same replies, cost nothing and are fast. It is git-ignored; a clean checkout starts empty (about 6 s per drafted ticket, so a 120-ticket run takes roughly 10-15 minutes).

## D-11 Output validation (2026-11-11, B-09)
- **Decision:** `src/validator.py`. A reply is sent only if every check passes; any failure blocks it and escalates with the reason recorded. Code checks: private data, commitment (promise, date, roadmap), billing wording, data-location/compliance claims, figures not in the sources or the customer's message, prompt leakage. Plus the PR-03 claim-support check by the model, which **fails closed** (a model failure blocks).
- **Why code checks beside the model:** the checker is the same small model that wrote the reply and can be too lenient (or, as seen, erratic). The red-team set (19 replies, one or more per check) is blocked in `tests/test_validator.py`.
- **Tuned on development only, two fixes after the first run** (first run blocked 11 of 40 drafts, 9 of them labelled answerable, including a claim copied word for word from the source): (1) inline step markers ("1. Open ...") were being read as figures; (2) a claim the model calls unsupported is accepted if at least 80% of its content words appear in the cited passages, and the override is recorded in the decision log. After the fixes: 35 answered, 25 escalated (5 of them blocked by the validator, 9 overrides).
- **Known limits, stated plainly:** (a) the 80% rule can accept a claim that rearranges source words into something the source does not say; (b) the claim check still blocks harmless introductory sentences (3 of 5 blocks), costing some automation but erring safe; (c) 5 of 35 answered tickets were labelled "not answerable" (14%), though the labels are not a function of the ticket text (see data_findings.md) and the replies read as grounded; (d) there is not yet an independent measure of hallucination (PR-04 judge, B-11), so the hallucination metric stays empty rather than being guessed.
- **Closes PRD open question 4 (wording checks for billing commitments and data residency).** The router no longer relies on intent rates alone for these.

## D-12 Failure handling (2026-11-11, B-17; FR-11, NFR-02, A11)
- **Decision:** every failure ends in a logged escalation, never a crash or a missing ticket. Added a **circuit breaker** to the model client (opens after 3 consecutive failed requests, fails fast for 30 s, one trial call after that). Without it each ticket in an outage spent about 7 s retrying, so a 120-ticket run would have taken over 10 minutes just to fail.
- **Found and fixed by the tests:** `DecisionLog.reconcile` raised if the log database became unreadable, which would have crashed the harness at the end of a run. It now returns `reconciles: false` with the error, and the harness exits 1, so the gap is visible without losing the results.
- **Induced conditions (`tests/test_failure_handling.py`, 21 tests):** provider unreachable (a real SDK connection to a closed port), timeouts, 429 rate limiting that never clears and that does clear, retrieval returning nothing or failing, classifier missing, a stage crashing on one ticket, decision log unwritable, 13 kinds of malformed record (None, strings, numbers, wrong types, unknown channel, empty/whitespace/unicode/control characters, 200 KB text, an injection attempt, bad timestamp), unreadable or odd input files, and everything at once.
- **Live check through the real command (2026-11-11):** `evaluation.harness` on 40 validation tickets with the model URL pointed at a dead port: 40 of 40 processed, 40 escalated (each with a template hand-over note), decision log reconciled, 31 s end to end.
- **Behaviour worth knowing:** a ticket file that is not valid JSON is read as a single malformed record and escalated rather than rejected, so nothing is silently dropped; a file that is empty or valid JSON that is not tickets (for example `42`) exits with code 2 and a clear message. Identical junk records get the same content-hash id by design (deterministic), and the log reconciles per ticket and stage.
- **Not covered:** a provider that returns plausible but wrong content is handled by the validator (D-11), not here; disk-full during result writing is not induced.

## D-13 Gate run result and what it says about the router threshold (2026-11-11, B-11)
- **Result:** see `docs/gate_run_validation.md`. Unattended validation run completed (80/80, reconciled, 0 always-escalate tickets answered); a renamed, label-free copy gave identical results. Validation run count: 1.
- **Measured pass-through of wrongly auto-routed tickets: 11 of 15 (73%)** against the 20% assumed in D-08. The validator is a weak filter for tickets the *labels* call not answerable, because their replies are grounded in the documentation. Implied threshold at W = 9 x 0.73 is 0.87.
- **Not applied yet, deliberately.** Using the validation figure to set the threshold would tune on the held-out set. The pass-through must be re-measured on development tickets (same code, a larger distinct-text sample than the 60 used so far) and the threshold re-fitted from that. Also open: whether W = 9 is the right cost. It is an assumption (Marcus: "I would rather it said nothing"), and raising the threshold to 0.87 would cut automation sharply; that trade-off is the author's call.
- **NFR-01 latency (p95 under 3 s) is not met** (p95 17.9 s). To be revised in the PRD with the numbers, not argued away.

## D-14 Independent quality check against the reference answers (2026-11-11)
- **Why:** D-13 found that the validator blocked 23 replies the labels call good and only 4 the labels call bad, so either the validator or the labels might be wrong. The reference answers (`ground_truth_responses.json`, 200 senior-agent answers) are independent of both.
- **Limit found first:** references exist **only for tickets the labels call answerable**, so none of the 29 label-defined "wrong answers that got through" has a reference. This check cannot say whether those 29 are wrong. It can only say whether replies to answerable tickets are good, and whether the validator withholds the weaker ones.
- **Method (`evaluation/quality_eval.py`, development tickets, 68 drafted replies with a reference):** cited document is an expected one; must-mention coverage (word-overlap text check); must-not-claim patterns (refund issued, fixed on our side, delivery date); MiniLM similarity to the reference.
- **Result:** 91.2% cite an expected document; 0 of 68 make a forbidden claim; similarity to reference 0.53; must-mention coverage 0.39 (crude text check, likely understated). Sent replies (n=58) vs replies the validator withheld (n=10): similarity 0.54 vs 0.50, must-mention 0.44 vs 0.25, expected-document 91% vs 90%. The withheld ones are slightly weaker, so the validator is not blocking at random, but n=10 is too small to rely on.
- **The model judge (PR-04 v0.2) failed and is discarded.** It reported a violation in 98.5% of replies; in 56 of 68 its "violated" list was just the MUST NOT CLAIM items copied back, while the pattern check found none. Its 0-5 scores split into 2s and 4s with no usable pattern. It is the same small model that wrote the replies. No conclusion in this project rests on it. Lesson for the report: a same-model judge on a small free model is not an instrument.
- **What is still not measured:** the hallucination rate (<=5% target) and citation accuracy. The Evaluation Framework requires human assessment, at least 50 responses and two assessors. A blinded sheet of 60 sent replies with their source articles was generated (copied to `human_review/human_review_sheet.csv` beside the project folder, key in `human_review_key.json`, seeded sample; also in `evaluation/results/dev_distinct_out/`) for the author and a second person to fill in. Until that is done the report must say the rate is unmeasured, not give a number.
- **Consequence for the router threshold (D-13):** unresolved. The measurements neither show that the 29 survivors are wrong nor that they are fine, so there is no evidence for moving the threshold from 0.64 to 0.89. That decision stays with the author.

## D-15 Fairness audit (2026-11-11, B-14; NFR-06)
- **Result:** `docs/fairness_audit.md`. On 216 development tickets no group gap is statistically supported, and the sample cannot show gaps are under 5 points either: the honest statement is "no evidence of a gap, not shown to be absent". Largest signals: answered share by tier (standard 47% vs enterprise 65%, p = 0.17), route agreement in Europe (55% vs 71-77%, p = 0.08). 28 comparisons, none under p = 0.05.
- **By design:** group fields are never decision inputs (`tests/test_fairness.py` fails if any decision module reads them), so the only route to a gap is ticket wording. This also means the system cannot treat enterprise customers or non-fluent writers differently on purpose.
- **Where more data would matter:** Europe's lower routing agreement may reflect its mix of residency and compliance questions rather than unfairness; this audit cannot separate the two. A human review of reply quality by group needs more than the 60 sampled replies.
- **Limit of the instrument:** the labels, not people, define "right answer" here, and the labels are noisy (see D-13, D-14).

## D-17 Routing threshold stays at 0.64 (2026-11-12, decided by the author)
- **Decision:** keep the threshold at 0.64. The cost-rule value with the measured pass-through (88% on development, 73% on validation) would be 0.87 to 0.90 and automate only 5-17% of tickets (D-13). The author chose not to apply it because the evidence does not show that the tickets that pass are actually wrong (D-14: references exist only for answerable tickets, the model judge failed).
- **What this means, to be stated in the report:** the 0.64 value was derived from a 20% pass-through assumption that measurement did not support. It is kept as a judgement call, not as a fitted optimum. The safety of automatic sending rests on the validator and the always-escalate rules, not on the threshold, and the hallucination rate is pending human review (blinded sheet of 60 replies). Not deployable on automatic sending until that review is done; shadow mode first (see docs/governance.md, declaration).

## D-19: Human review of 60 replies (one assessor): hallucination rate and a citation defect

**Method.** 60 replies drafted for development tickets and passed by the validator, blinded (ticket, reply, cited article only), reviewed 2026-11-12 by one assessor (HKP, the author). Responses: `docs/human_review_assessor1.csv`.

**Result.**
- Unsupported claims: 0 of 60 replies (95% Wilson upper bound 6.0%). Under the Evaluation Framework's target this passes, but the sample is small and these replies had already passed the validator, so it measures what gets through, not the model's raw rate.
- Citation correct: 49 of 60 (81.7%, 95% CI 70-89%). In all 11 "no" cases the citation was missing from the reply text: the id was in the metadata `citations` field but the model had not written `[DOC-...]` in the reply the customer sees. Reviewer notes also flagged two replies (R10, R17) with a needless extra sentence (verbosity, not a factual fault).

**Limits.** One assessor, so no inter-rater agreement; the Evaluation Framework asks for two. The author reviewed their own system's output, which can bias. Development tickets only; the held-out set was not used.

**Fix.** `draft_answer` now appends `[DOC-...]` for each cited id when the reply text has none (test added; 194 tests pass). This is deterministic; the cited ids were already validated against the retrieved passages. Effect on the rate is to be re-measured by a re-run and re-review of the 11 replies.
